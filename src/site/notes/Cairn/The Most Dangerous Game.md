Warwick grumbles in his luxurious armchair, plush red velvet doing little to soothe his inner pains. The embarrassment from his latest book reading proves almost too much to bear. With magnanimous verbosity and dogmatic passion he enthralled the gathered crowd with passages from his latest adventure memoir ‘_Check Mate! How To Corner and Conquer Any Queen_’, when a heckling jeer rose from the crowd, pointing out presumed discrepancies and fallacies in the chess metaphors the book was rife with. Within minutes his questionable knowledge of the game of chess was all anyone could talk about, and Warwick had to cut his planned 3 hour monologue 2 hours and 54 minutes short. Distasteful! Disheartening! Disconcerting! In other words: unacceptable!

Swigging deep from a decanter of amber ambrosia, Warwick canters through his study. Something in the back of his mind tickles at an answer, a solution, a way to say nay to the nay-sayers!

Ah, there.

An old scroll addressed to a copper merchant of some sorts, pilfered from historical archives that Warwick bravely explored, eluding capture by many guards stating ‘he’s not supposed to be here’ and ‘sir this is a government building’. A document of tremendous historical value for sure, but the contents of the document – complaints about the quality of the copper – are not what draws Warwick to the dried parchment. It’s the intended purpose of the copper and the name of the disgruntled author: Lord Chamberlain Brink requested the copper for the construction of the world’s most magnificent chess set.

Consulting his ledgers and annals, Warwick confirms his hunches that the complaint towards the copper merchant was the final straw in a series of events that culminated in a civil war in the area, just over a century ago. Apparently the position of Lord Chamberlain was left vacant following Brink’s demise. 'Thisly and thusly,' Warwick reasons, 'the Chamberlain’s Palace must still stand vacant, and the fabled chess set should be found within!' 

Claiming this treasure would surely put shame to anyone who claims Warwick has no familiarity with the game! And Warwick knows just who to ask for help in this archeological adventure…
