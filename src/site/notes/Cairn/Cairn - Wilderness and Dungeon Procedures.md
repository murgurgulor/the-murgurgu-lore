## Wilderness Travel
Traveling overland in civilized areas is narrated briefly by the GM, unless the players want to focus on specific elements.

Traveling through the wilderness requires navigation and exploration, and therefore has a risk of getting lost, encountering setbacks, and meeting nature's inhabitants.

In Wilderness Travel, time is measured in **Watches**. There are four Watches to a day, each around 6 hours long.
- The **Dawn Watch** is from **Sunrise** until **Midday**. 
- The **Noon Watch** is from **Midday** until **Sunset**.
- The **Dusk Watch** is from **Sunset** until **Midnight**.
- The **Night Watch** is during the dark hours.

Usually three Watches in the day are spent with activities, and the Night Watch is spent resting. Characters can always choose to do things differently, but if they go 4 consecutive Watches without properly resting, they become **Deprived** and take **Strain**.


---
When you travel through the Wilderness, you usually follow a trail, landmark, or map. If you don't know where your destination is on the map, you might have to spend time exploring areas to find it. 

The time it takes to travel between two locations is measured in Watches of Hiking. This is a composite of distance, terrain type, trail quality (if any), and circumstancial factors like the weather. If you spend a Watch doing something other than Hiking, you won't progress to your destination. 

Normally people travel for 2 Watches in a day, spending the remainder of the daylight foraging or setting up camp. If someone tells you something is 'x days travel away', multiply the given number of days by 2 to get the amount of Watches you need to Hike to get there - barring any unforeseen circumstances or delays.


#### Wilderness Cycle
Each Watch of Wilderness Travel, the following Cycle of events happens:
1. The GM describes the current environment and rehashes the party's situation, giving any pertinent information that is immediately noticeable to the characters.
2. At **Sunrise** and **Sunset**, the GM rolls the **Wilderness Event Die** to see if any unexpected events will transpire in the coming 12 hours. If the party elects to Hike, Explore, or Forage during the Night Watch, an additional Event is rolled.
3. The players each declare what **Wilderness Action** their characters will be doing during the Watch. 
4. The GM narrates the Watch's happenings, and incorporates the result of the Wilderness Event Die to which the player characters can respond.
5. At the end of the Watch, any resources are checked against their duration and inventory is updated. If the party hasn't reached their destination, the Cycle begins anew.


---
#### Wilderness Actions
For each Watch the characters declare which **Wilderness Action** they will take. Only the **Hike** action actually covers ground, at the end of the other actions you remain (roughly) in your current location.


##### Hike
- A Wilderness Action that has to be taken as a group. When you Hike, you move closer to your destination.
- Designate one of the Hikers to be the **Wayfinder**. They will roll to see if you get lost along the way. The chances of getting lost are dependent on the terrain, path quality, and familiarity or skill of the Wayfinder in the current environment. If you get lost, you will have to spend another Watch Hiking to get back on track or you end up at a different place than you were aiming for. 
- Other factors can also limit your progress, such as inclement weather or unexpected blocks or overgrowths. 
- While you Hike, any **Landmarks** are revealed according to their distance.
- Hiking during the Night Watch comes with added risk of encountering dangers, allows for less progress, and requires proper light sources to do. It is possible, but not without risk and cost!

##### Explore
- A character who Explores spends the Watch searching around in a large area, looking for any **Hidden** features or scouting out the terrain ahead. 
- If your destination is not marked on your map, the only way to find it is to Hike in the right direction and spend time Exploring the area.
- If you Explore, you don't move from your current location.

##### Forage
The Forage Action lets you search around the area for food, water, or specific plants or herbs you can use for medicine or other purposes. How much you find is determined by the specific Terrain Type of the environment you're in. At the end of the Watch, roll the indicated die to see how much you find.

For every additional ally that Forages at the same time, step the die size up by one (so 1 > d3 > d4 > d6 > d8 > d10 > d12). Characters who are particularly skilled at foraging in a certain terrain can also increase the die size at the GM's discretion.

| Terrain Type        | Forage (in uses of Rations) | Typically Used Plants and Herbs |
| ------------------- | --------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Desert              | 1                           | 0                               |
| Mountains           | 1                           | 1                               |
| Hills               | 1d3                         | 1                               |
| Plains              | 1d3                         | 1d3                             |
| Light Forest        | 1d4                         | 1d3                             |
| Swamp/Marshes       | 1d3                         | 1d4                             |
| Dense Forest/Jungle | 1d4                         | 1d4                             |


##### Make Camp
- When you make camp, you set out to find a good location to rest in. This includes scouting for the right area, gathering firewood, cooking, setting up tents, clearing the perimeter, et cetera. Only one person is needed to Make Camp for the whole party.
- You need to have Made Camp in order to **Sleep**. Otherwise, you can only **Rest**.

##### Rest
- If you spend a Watch Resting, you remain in your current location and only undertake light activities.
- If you consume a **Ration**, you recover one Strain at the end of the Watch. If you can't consume a **Ration**, you don't gain any benefits from Resting.

##### Sleep
- If you consume a **Ration** and spend a Watch Sleeping without any major interruptions, you recover all Strain when you wake up. Additionally you can recover 1 point of Quality Loss. If your sleep is majorly interrupted or if you can't consume a **Ration**, you don't gain any benefits.
- You can only Sleep if you have Made Camp. Otherwise the Watch counts as a Rest.
- It's generally advised to set a sentry while sleeping in the wild. That person might need to Rest earlier, while others Make Camp or Forage.

## The Point of Traveling 
When you travel, you move between two Points on the map. A Point is a place that is noteworthy, valuable, or otherwise important to travelers. Points can range from towns to dungeons, and from crossroads to unique environmental features. 

To know where you can travel to, you need to know of a Point. Knowledge of Points are divided in three levels:
- **Landmark** Points are always known - either because they're common knowledge, they're on your map, or because they're readily visible from a distance away. You don't need to spend time or effort to find a Landmark Point that you don't know of yet, if you Hike near it, you automatically discover it.
- **Hidden** Points are not always known, but can be easily discovered through Exploration in the right area or by gathering information from people in the know. They're noticeable enough to be found with a general surveying of the area, but not obvious enough to be a Landmark. A common way to find Hidden Points is to seek out elevation to broaden your horizons.
- **Secret** Points are like Hidden Points, but they can only be discovered if you actually know what to look for. You'll need a map or otherwise be privy to information from elsewhere to find it.

#### Example of Wilderness Travel
The party starts out at Auchentyr and wants to travel to the Wood Lodge, which is 4 Watches ('2 days travel') away. 
On day one, the party elects to Hike for the Dawn and Noon Watch. For the Dusk Watch, they decide to Make Camp so they can Sleep in the Night Watch. They have now covered 2 Watches of the trip and have 2 Watches to go until their destination. 
The following day, the weather turns bad. The party spends the Dawn Watch Hiking, but unfortunately get lost. They have to spend the Noon Watch to get back on track. They now have a choice: Make Camp to be ready for the Night Watch and cover the remaining distance tomorrow, or risk Hiking through the Dusk Watch and arrive at the Lodge late at night.

##### Wilderness Events
When the characters travel through the wilderness, they might come across unexpected obstacles, natural phenomena, or wilderness inhabitants. The GM rolls the Wilderness Event Die - a d6 - at **Sunrise** and **Sunset**. The results of the Die occur somewhere in the 12 hours thereafter. If the party decides to Hike, Explore, or Forage during the Night Watch, an additional Event Die is rolled.

| Wilderness Event Die | Event Type                                                                                                                                                                |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1                    | A **Wandering Encounter** comes across the company. They will have to deal with it in some way before proceeding.                                                         |
| 2                    | You find a **Sign, Omen, or Track** that hints at an impending Encounter, Event, or that reveals signs of a nearby feature or location.                                   |
| 3                    | **Altered Circumstances** indirectly affect the company's progress. The weather shifts, a resource is lost, or something else happens outside of the company's influence. |
| 4                    | The **Environment** proves bothersome: something is blocking the way forward, a hazard needs to be traversed, or your chances of getting lost are increased.              |
| 5                    | You have **Quiet** travels                                                                                                                                                |
| 6                    | You **Discover** something useful along the way. This can include supplies, shortcuts, hidden points of interest, or otherwise beneficial happenstances.                  |

## Weather
Weather is determined on the season. Every day at the start of the Dawn Watch, the GM rolls to see how the weather changes for that day. Altered Circumstances can prompt a further shift in the weather. Weather is categorized as Fair, Unpleasant, Inclement, and Extreme.

- **Fair** weather is a broad category, from clear and sunny skies to light rains and wind. It may not be pleasant, but it doesn't tax you in your travels.
- **Unpleasant** weather - such as heavy rains, icy winds, or sweltering heat - may wear you out. If you Hike, Explore, or Forage in Unpleasant weather you have to make a VIG Save at the end of the Watch or gain 1 Strain. Your chance to get lost increases while Hiking in Unpleasant weather.
- In **Inclement** weather - such as thunderstorms, blizzards, and heat waves - it's best to hunker down. You always take Strain if you Hike, Explore, or Forage. Some types of terrain become unpassable in Inclement weather, other ones are significantly harder to get through.
- In **Extreme** weather - hurricanes, flooding, extreme temperatures - you can't do anything but shelter.

## Dungeon Crawling
When the characters explore a dungeon or a dungeon-like environment, they follow a cyclical process: the **Dungeon Cycle**. Each Dungeon Cycle, the characters can choose to move, explore, search, or rest in the dungeon, with the following sequence of play:

1. The GM describes the current environment and rehashes the party's situation, giving any pertinent information that is immediately noticeable to the characters.
2. The players each declare what activity their characters will be doing during the Cycle. Examples of common activities are:
	- Moving through a **Flux Way**
	- Searching a room for secrets (particularly large rooms may require you to state a specific area to search)
	- Traversing a series of previously explored rooms
	- Keep an eye out for danger
	- Overcome an obstacle, such as a locked door or a trap
	- Fight monsters or bad guys
3. Once everyone has declared their activity, all activities are resolved simultaneously.
4. The GM determines whether there is a trigger to roll for a **Dungeon Event** on this Cycle, and resolves the outcome.
5. Any resources are checked against their duration and inventory is updated, and the Cycle repeats.

A Dungeon Cycle can take a variable amount of time, but for purposes of time-keeping one Cycle is considered to last for 10 minutes.


### Dungeon Events
When the characters move through the Dungeon, they might attract unwanted attention or cause the dungeon to change in some way. The **Dungeon Event Die** - a d6 - is a tool for the GM to determine when and how these dynamics change.

The GM rolls the Dungeon Event Die in the following situations:
- You travel to a new level or zone of the dungeon
- You traverse a sequence of previously explored locations
- You linger for longer than a Cycle in an unsafe location
- You rest in an unsafe location
- You make a disturbance or otherwise cause a ruckus


| Event Die | Outcome                                                                                                                                                                                |
| --------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1         | A **Wandering Encounter** is presented to the party. They will have to deal with it in a direct way before proceeding.                                                                 |
| 2         | A **Sign, Omen, or Track** that hints at an impending Encounter or Event, or that reveals a secret, gives a hint about what has happened before, or what will happen soon.             |
| 3         | **Altered Circumstances** indirectly affect the party. These can be environmental changes or proceeding events that transpire out of the direct knowledge and perception of the party. |
| 4         | An **Unfortunate Incident** befalls the Company that provides a momentary setback, faltering resources, or that complicates their situation                                            |
| 5         | All is **Quiet** for now                                                                                                                                                               |
| 6         | The Company **Discovers** something useful.                                                                                                                                            |

### Flux Ways
Some dungeons are not simply a string of rooms connected together. They may have long or confusing paths, or require dedicated exploring or backtracking to navigate. These areas are known as **Flux Ways**. The first time a Flux Way is entered, it takes a set number of Cycles to explore. For each Cycle it takes to traverse the Flux Way, the GM rolls on an Event table specific to that Flux Way. The events need to be resolved before the company can exit the Flux Way at the other end. Once explored, Flux Ways are easily traversed.


### Resting in a Dungeon
If the party wants to recover **Fatigue** while in a dungeon, they need to rest for a full Cycle. If they rest in an unsafe location in the dungeon, the GM will roll for a Dungeon Event. Any results come to pass at the end of the Cycle.


### Light in a Dungeon
If a dungeon has no light sources of its own, the party will need to carry at least **one light source per three characters** that travel close together. If there are not enough light sources to support the number of characters in the party, the characters furthest away from the light source are at a severe disadvantage. If someone without a Torch goes ahead of others, they will be left in the dark.

A regular Torch has 3 usage dots. Every 6 Dungeon Cycles of use, mark one usage dot on the Torch. You also mark one usage dot whenever you have to relight a Torch that was extinguished, either by choice or by bad luck.
